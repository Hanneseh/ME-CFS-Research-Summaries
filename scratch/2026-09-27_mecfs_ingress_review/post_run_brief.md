This run adds a reason to keep watching a low-burden oral treatment, but no near-term breakthrough for a very severely affected adult who is bedbound except for toilet visits. Compared with baseline `672a1231717f`, the treatment evidence remains uncertain, and the clearest practical addition concerns nutritional care rather than recovery from ME/CFS.

Bezisterim is the treatment development most worth following. BioVie’s randomized Long COVID trial found no statistically significant endpoint across the full population, while prespecified high-symptom-burden subgroups showed exploratory signals. Those results come from a sponsor announcement, with many outcomes and no reported multiplicity correction. The registry excluded CFS/POTS/fibromyalgia predating COVID; post-COVID ME/CFS was not automatically excluded, but ME/CFS-specific results and bedbound inclusion are unknown. Oral dosing would be relatively low burden if efficacy were eventually confirmed; study visits and cognitive testing still carry burden. A prospectively specified confirmatory trial, with credible PEM/function outcomes and severe-patient inclusion, would materially change confidence. Phase 3 is planned without a firm result date, so a 1–3-year horizon is uncertain and there is no current treatment recommendation. Separately, the adjacent fibromyalgia trial of the IgG-lowering drug rozanolixizumab adds caution: it found no meaningful broad benefit despite meeting a lenient statistical threshold. That result does not establish what antibody-directed treatment can do in ME/CFS. [BioVie results](https://investors.bioviepharma.com/news/news-details/2026/BioVie-Announces-Topline-Results-from-Phase-2-ADDRESSLC-Trial-Evaluating-Bezisterim-for-the-Treatment-of-Neurological-Symptoms-Associated-with-Long-COVID/default.aspx), [ADDRESS-LC registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06847191), [fibromyalgia trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(26)00252-3).

Genetics gained useful evidence and a clearer unresolved problem. A biobank preprint reports seven loci replicated between cohorts, but none across all three cohorts and no overlap with DecodeME. This does not negate DecodeME or confirm it; differences in patient ascertainment may contribute, and independent work must reconcile the signals. A separate small prospective infection cohort found that depression and CFS polygenic scores did not predict post-infective fatigue, without excluding modest effects. These studies improve biological orientation rather than identify a treatment or a clinically usable responder group. Severe-patient representation was not reported. The next meaningful step is replication with clear ME/CFS/PEM phenotyping and severity reporting; clinical translation has an indeterminate horizon. [Biobank preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.09.26362115), [prospective polygenic-score study](https://doi.org/10.1017/thg.2026.10098).

The BACME tube-feeding survey is the most directly relevant severe-care addition. It documents delayed nutritional support, difficulties continuing feeding at home, and the need for adaptations to standard care. Severe patients helped design it, but the respondent severity breakdown is unknown; it is a small, self-selected service-experience survey, not evidence of disease-modifying benefit or a comparison of feeding methods. Its practical importance is recognizing malnutrition and arranging individualized support while accounting for PEM, sensory intolerance, positioning, tube changes, and monitoring. BACME intends to develop guidance, without a stated timetable. Better prospective care-outcome data would strengthen confidence. This is relevant to care conversations now, not an instruction to start tube feeding. [Primary survey report](https://meassociation.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/BACME-MECFS-Tube-Feeding-Survey-Report-Sept-2026.pdf).

Read **Emerging Pharmacotherapy**, **Care & Assessment for Severe ME/CFS**, and **Genetic Risk Factors** first in Source Control. The remaining diffs cover the published remdesivir feasibility study (uncontrolled, five IV treatment days, high-PEM-risk exclusion), a negative circulating-NETosis result, CPET/coronary physiology, and clinical measurement. The integrated-care publication updates an existing conference account and reports no 12-month physical-function advantage; completing rehabilitation does not establish PEM safety. [Remdesivir publication](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfimm/iqag014), [integrated-care publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amjmed.2026.09.013).

Did this run add a credible reason for hope for this patient? **A small, conditional reason to keep following bezisterim and a concrete improvement in attention to severe-care needs—yes. A demonstrated treatment capable of changing this patient’s function or disease course—no.** The uncertainty about very severe inclusion remains central.
